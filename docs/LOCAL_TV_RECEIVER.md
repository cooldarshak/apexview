# Free local Pitwall TV receiver

Install the same signed Pitwall TV APK on phone and Android/Google TV. Open **Settings → TV casting → Receive from phone** on the TV. A receiver button is also available before sign-in: clear media is authorized and prepared by the signed-in phone, without copying its account tokens to the TV. Keep the receiver screen open.

On the phone, open compatible archive feeds, choose the desired layout, tap the player **Cast** button and choose the discovered **Pitwall TV** device. Accept **Allow** on the TV. No paid developer account, hosted receiver, IP entry or pairing code is required. Both devices must be on a LAN permitting multicast discovery and device-to-device traffic. Guest-network isolation can prevent discovery/playback.

## Playback and quality

The phone packages all selected clear HEVC sources as one HLS atlas and relays the selected audio. It requests at least 720p and verifies actual source display dimensions. A 540p phone composition is rebuilt rather than reused. Single and Multiple decoder preferences both use this shared casting preparation. 1080p is preferred when requested and compatible; the source/combined stream and TV codec must support the resulting dimensions and rate. Failure reports an error, without silent 540p fallback. Small TV windows naturally occupy fewer display pixels than their source resolution.

The TV has exactly one ExoPlayer with one hardware video renderer, not one player per window. Its codec selector retains normal audio decoders, including software AAC on TVs. `SingleDecoderSceneView` samples regions of that single decoded HEVC picture. The sender transmits the canvas, fixed source mapping, coded/display dimensions, x/y/width/height and z order. This supports main-left/three-right, Grid, saved positions, movement, resize and overlap. The complete phone video canvas is fitted to the TV without changing its proportions. Moving a window updates geometry without another decoder or re-encoding.

After the TV renders its first GLES frame, phone video/audio tracks are disabled and local players paused. Phone controls follow the receiver clock instead of a still-running phone player. The phone remains the media relay and must stay on the local network. An explicit connected-device foreground service, with a Stop casting notification and CPU/Wi-Fi locks, keeps the LAN connection and producer alive while the phone display is off or another app is foreground. It releases those locks and the notification on disconnect/exit; no phone video/audio tracks need to run for this service. Pause, resume, seek, speed, selected audio and mute target the TV. Back/exit/disconnect releases receiver playback and returns phone playback to the latest TV position, under the saved decoder mode. TV D-pad OK pauses/resumes; left/right seek ten seconds. Feed selection changes prepare a replacement relay while retaining the previous relay and keeping phone tracks stopped; failed preparation can be retried.

The Google Cast path shares the local-track handoff, quality floor and phone controller clock. Default Media Receiver still shows the encoded atlas. Optional registered Google layout receiver remains supported, but the free native receiver needs no Google registration. Google Cast acknowledges media loading rather than providing the native GLES frame acknowledgement.

The phone also sends the current native timing rows, weather/flags, driver positions and Track/Edge/Pit map state. The TV reuses the same drawing components and panel coordinates without loading timing archives or allocating another decoder. Position samples interpolate against the TV clock; table snapshots update independently and are reused between position frames. Driver selection on the TV is returned to the phone. Timing geometry and state messages are bounded and chunked within FCast framing; missing or malformed timing updates do not stop video. Full driver-card/radio panels are not included. Video timing/data/map channels selected as feeds are included like other video sources. For two through six explicit channels, protected/archive and live offers now use a separate decoder-free rolling CMAF producer. It preserves every source tile, the original CBCS encrypted payloads and encryption records, and relays the selected original CMAF audio. It requires actual full-feed HD dimensions and a common rendition fitting the atlas budget; it never calls a partial source tile a full 720p feed. The existing tile-selection POC remains available for local diagnostics.

The protected receiver must be signed in to F1 TV on that TV. After Allow it obtains its own entitlement and authorized multifeed licence endpoint for the requested content/channel IDs, then Media3 obtains the Widevine licence on the TV. Phone tokens, authorization headers, licence responses, keys and decrypted samples never cross the LAN. One ExoPlayer carries composed video and audio. L3 uses the ordinary scene renderer when the licence permits it; actual L1/unknown level uses a secure SurfaceView and protected EGL context/window. Missing OEM protected-texture support reports an error without an insecure output fallback. Software DRM remains the TV's existing preference, rather than a forced promise of L3.

Live capture follows contiguous published segments, including after a short pause. Archive seek and selected-audio changes prepare a new aligned window while retaining the previous relay until acknowledgement. The receiver reports controller time including the sliding MPD window offset; transient licence loading reports buffering rather than ended. Live starts and seeks retain the requested time within the published DVR window; expired content reports an error. Go Live reloads the current live edge. Single-channel handoff uses the TV's own authorized stream and actual HD track selection, retaining the same phone window and native overlays. The single feed uses a regular secure SurfaceView when necessary; multiple protected picture regions use protected EGL. No phone-supplied URL is accepted for direct TV playback. Actual F1 licence/secure-GPU acceptance, audio synchronization, sustained playback and Miracast routing on the target devices still require device verification.

## FCast interoperability

This is an independent implementation of the documented **FCast v3 JSON/TCP core**, not a bundled stock FCast app or a claim of full v4 conformance. It advertises `_fcast._tcp.` through Android NSD, with TXT `version=3` and `pitwallScene=1`. TCP normally uses 46899; a free port is advertised if occupied. Phone discovery resolves sequentially for older Android implementations and selects IPv4, matching the phone relay's address family.

Frames use a four-byte little-endian size including the one-byte opcode, followed by optional UTF-8 JSON; lengths outside 1..32000 are rejected before allocation. Implemented core messages: Version, Initial, Play, Pause, Resume, Stop, Seek, PlaybackUpdate, VolumeUpdate, SetVolume, PlaybackError, SetSpeed, Ping, Pong and PlayUpdate. Playlist/event subscriptions, mirroring/WebRTC, TLS/FlatBuffers v4, and general internet-URL receiver use are not implemented. Stock FCast senders may use this receiver's limited LAN media playback core; stock receivers cannot interpret Pitwall layouts and are not offered as layout destinations.

Initial messages from both Pitwall peers contain `pitwallScene: 1`. Standard `Play.metadata.custom.pitwallScene` carries scene version 1; `playbackId` identifies the specific media load. Only after negotiation are private opcode 128 (scene update JSON) and 129 (rendered scene acknowledgement with playbackId/sources) exchanged. Receivers advertise `pitwallWidevine: 1` for the independently authorized protected path. `Play.metadata.custom.pitwallLicense` contains only content ID, channel IDs and selected audio ID; `pitwallStream` supplies the public media clock origin/duration. Peers also negotiate `pitwallOverlays: 1` before using private opcode 130 for bounded geometry/table/position messages and the drawn acknowledgement, and 131 for driver-selection controls. Unknown optional opcodes are ignored, never treated as controls. A stale first-frame acknowledgement cannot hand off a newer load.

The TV requires Allow for each connection before executing media controls. Only one sender is accepted. Packet size, write queues, receive timeout and media buffer lengths are bounded. Media/subresources must use the connected phone's numeric local address, same scheme and port; redirects, remote authorization headers and inline manifests are rejected. HTTP cleartext is enabled for this local clear-media relay; F1 HTTPS authorization stays on the phone. This v3 LAN transport is not encrypted and is intended for a trusted local network. Closing/backgrounding the receiver stops advertising, sockets and playback.

Protocol reference: https://docs.fcast.org/protocol/v3/ . FCast project and licensing: https://github.com/futo-org/fcast (MIT). No FCast implementation code was copied or dependency added. NSD: https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/wifi/use-nsd .

## Miracast / Samsung Smart View

The Cast picker and Settings provide **Screen mirroring / Smart View**, which opens Android's public `ACTION_CAST_SETTINGS` when available and otherwise explains Samsung's Quick Settings → Smart View entry. Miracast source/sink negotiation is provided by the phone and TV firmware, not an APK-level private API.

While this connection is requested, Pitwall listens to the selected `MediaRouter` live-video route. If Android exposes a distinct Presentation display, the app moves its existing player surfaces there and replaces the phone picture with a controller banner. It fits the same main-plus-three video canvas and native Track/Edge/Pit/tower overlays to the TV. It does not create a second set of players. Closing/disconnecting that display returns surfaces to the phone and restores the saved quality cap. Background/PiP handling preserves playback for this explicit external-display session.

The app requires real source renditions of at least 720p and checks the exposed display mode; a lower-resolution clear single-decoder session is rebuilt. L1 output requires both secure-display/protected-buffer flags and a secure window. Output denied by the OS or content policy stays denied. If the manufacturer only provides ordinary mirroring, phone video remains visible because it is the TV's source. Miracast still decodes on the phone and the OS negotiates the final wireless encoding resolution/bitrate. The app cannot guarantee that final encoding is 720p or force a TV firmware capability. Independent TV decoding and fully stopped phone media tracks use the native Pitwall receiver.

References: https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/Presentation , https://developer.android.com/reference/android/provider/Settings#ACTION_CAST_SETTINGS and https://www.samsung.com/us/support/troubleshoot/TSG10001542/ .

## Verification and remaining device work

JVM regression coverage includes real socket control framing/order/close, oversized/truncated packet rejection, media-origin restrictions, main-plus-three scene round trips and overlap, coded padding, below-720 rejection, a remote clock independent of phone playback, actual Media3 track disabling/restoration in a FeedPlayer, and retaining software AAC while rejecting software video fallback. Existing independent HEVC decode/pixel comparisons and receiver JavaScript tests remain in CI. These checks do not prove TV hardware playback.

Install the signed update on S22 Ultra and TCL 55C655. Enable diagnostic logs, open receiver on TV, select four archive feeds on phone and connect. Confirm each feed's identity/main-plus-three arrangement, then move/resize/overlap windows. Turn on the native timing tower and each Track/Edge/Pit map mode, resize and move their panels, seek and pause, and confirm flags/rows/positions match the phone controller time. Look for `TIMING_UI_DRAWN` on the TV and `TIMING_UI_CONFIRMED` on the phone. Confirm phone picture/audio stop and play/pause/seek/speed/audio/mute still operate the TV. Confirm disconnect restores the latest position without reviving extra decoders in Single mode. Confirm discovery, permission refusal, reconnect, changing feeds, background/exit, receiver 720p/1080p limits and sustained playback. Look for `CAST_SOURCE_QUALITY`, `PitwallReceiver VIDEO_DECODER_INIT active=1`, `SCENE_RENDERING_CONFIRMED`, `LOCAL_PLAYBACK_STOPPED` and scene frame counters in Download/Pitwall. Device results remain pending until those runs are observed.
